*For two years the scarce input in AI moved down the physical stack — chips, then memory, then power. This week it jumped the other direction, all the way up, to the one input no foundry can fabricate: investable capital. SpaceX prices Friday; OpenAI and Anthropic are in the queue behind it. Meanwhile the macro coin everyone's been staring at finally lands at 8:30 this morning.*

## 🔎 The Lead: $4 Trillion of New AI Equity Is About to Drink From One Glass

Tech kept bleeding into Wednesday. Tuesday the Nasdaq fell **0.97% to 25,678.82** and the S&P slipped **0.26% to 7,386.65**, while the Dow actually *rose* **86 points to 50,872.11** — the same split tape, tech down and the old economy up, that's been running all week. This morning futures point lower again: Nasdaq futures off more than **1%**, S&P futures down **~0.8%**, Dow futures near **50,561 (−0.68%)**. The headline reason on every screen is "rotation out of AI." The Lens asks what is actually being rationed underneath that word.

Here's the answer, and it's a new one. **SpaceX prices its Nasdaq debut Friday (June 12)** at **$135 a share, a $1.77 trillion valuation** — selling roughly **555 million shares for ~$75–80 billion in fresh cash**, at a sliver of free float (~3%). Behind it: **OpenAI** has confidentially filed for a listing as soon as September at **$730–850B**, and **Anthropic** filed its S-1 on June 1 targeting October at **~$965B**. That is well over **$4 trillion of new AI-and-space equity** lining up to be issued into the *same* institutional pool that currently funds Nvidia, the memory complex, and the power names. Crypto has already felt the suction — more than **$600B shed in a single week** as capital migrates toward the capital-intensive AI build.

Run it through the Lens. We've spent months tracking the scarce *physical* input as it moved down the stack — silicon to HBM to wafers to gigawatts (**Thesis 2: the scarce input keeps moving down the stack**). The twist this week is that the binding constraint jumped to the *top* of the stack, to the one input that is genuinely finite on a quarterly horizon: **the market's willingness and capacity to fund the build.** You cannot fab more aggregate equity demand. When $75B of brand-new AI exposure gets issued Friday — and index mechanics force funds to *buy* the new entrant on inclusion — the cash has to come from somewhere, and "somewhere" is the existing AI names already in the index. The rotation isn't sentiment. It's plumbing: the supply of investable AI exposure is flooding, so the price of *already-listed* AI exposure gets marked down to make room. This is the first time the bottleneck we track isn't a thing you mine, fab, or generate — it's the capital itself.

And one layer further down, who quietly owns *that* chokepoint? Not the issuers. The **listing venues and index machinery** — the rails every one of these megadeals has to cross. Nasdaq literally loosened its inclusion rules to land SpaceX faster, because the exchange earns its listing and data tolls regardless of which direction the AI trade breaks. In a gold rush you sell picks; in an IPO rush you own the turnstile. I'm naming that as the read, not the trade — see below for why today is the wrong day to act on anything.

## 📉 The Coin Lands at 8:30: CPI Resolves, Then Warsh

The number the whole tape has been coiled around prints **this morning, 8:30 ET: May CPI.** Consensus is **+0.5% month-over-month and 4.2% year-over-year** headline — which, if it lands, would be the hottest annual reading **since April 2023 (4.9%)**, up from April's **3.8%**. Core is seen at **+0.3% / 2.9%**. This is a pure denominator event: a hot print hands **Kevin Warsh cover to hike into 2027** at his first FOMC (June 16–17) and re-marks every long-duration asset down again; a soft one snaps the long-end repricing back. You could not pay me to guess it — it's a coin you don't own, and the entire index is currently a leveraged bet on its landing. **GE Vernova (GEV) stays open**, and it's the clean illustration of why I keep separating the two legs: this print moves the *macro* leg (the discount rate pressing GEV's price through ~4.56% 10-year yields), while every *physical* tripwire on the position — turbine cancellations, nuclear PPAs slipping, interconnection queues clearing — remains untriggered. The coin re-prices the denominator. It does not touch the build.

Watch the reflex in gold for the same lesson: bullion has slid toward **~$4,300**, erasing its year-to-date gain, not because the metal changed but because the *price of money* might. When even gold trades the discount rate, you know the day's dominant force is the denominator, not any business.

## 🛢️ Hormuz: The First Real Test of a Tripwire I Set

Oil broke from the macro stillness, and this one matters because it pokes directly at a tripwire I wrote down. WTI fell **3.4% to $88.20**, Brent **−2.97% to $91.45**, after US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said Strait of Hormuz ship traffic is "**rising very meaningfully.**" My standing invalidation on **Thesis 4 (energy as a geopolitical control variable)** is explicit: the thesis weakens on *a verified return of Hormuz transit to pre-war volumes — throughput, not a headline.* So is this it?

Not yet — and the honesty is the point. Wright's "meaningfully" is a claim, not a verified throughput figure; much of the flow that *is* moving runs with transponders dark (an estimated ~2M bpd slipping out unseen), which is the opposite of verifiable. In the same breath Trump said a deal was "two or three days away" — and then accused Iran of downing a US Apache patrolling the strait. A pause in strikes plus an official's adjective is not a lane reopened. So I log this as the **first data point toward the Thesis 4 tripwire**, not the trigger: the clock is now running on whether "rising meaningfully" becomes *measured at pre-war volumes* in the tanker data over the next weeks. The bottleneck is still the lane, not the barrel — but for the first time the lane is arguably loosening, and intellectual honesty means saying so out loud and watching it.

Watch the measured volumes, not the adjectives.

## 🧠 One Thought

The AI build has spent two years teaching everyone to look *down* — past the GPU to the memory, past the memory to the wafer, past the wafer to the gigawatt. The instinct became reflexive: whatever's scarce, look one layer lower for the real constraint. This week the constraint did something it hadn't before. It climbed *up* — out of the physical world entirely — and lodged at the top of the stack, in the supply of capital willing to fund the whole edifice. SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic aren't competing for chips or power with each other; they're competing for the same finite pool of institutional dollars, and the moment they draw from it, the names already holding that capital get repriced to make room.

That's a more dangerous kind of scarcity than a wafer shortage, because physical bottlenecks reprice *one thing* — memory, magnets, the lane. A capital bottleneck reprices *everything at once*, the same way a discount-rate shock does, which is exactly what makes this week rhyme with last week's CPI dread. Power is still the constraint on running the models; capital is now the constraint on *building the companies* — and for the first time those two scarcities are pulling in different directions. The crowd will spend this morning watching one number decide the price of money. The thing worth watching is slower and bigger: whether the world can actually *fund* the build it has already promised to power.
