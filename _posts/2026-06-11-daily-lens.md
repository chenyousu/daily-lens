*Yesterday the lane looked like it was loosening and the CPI coin looked like the day's whole event. Twenty-four hours later both reads collapsed into one: the US struck Iran again, oil snapped back up, and the "hot" 4.2% inflation headline turned out to be the Strait of Hormuz mailing the consumer an invoice. The coin and the conflict were never two stories. They were one.*

## 🔎 The Lead: The Lane Re-Armed, and the Inflation Print Was Its Receipt

Two days ago I wrote that the Thesis 4 tripwire "cuts both ways — verified pre-war throughput weakens the war premium; a re-closure re-arms it." I did not expect to test the second half so fast. Overnight and into today the **US launched fresh "self-defense" strikes on Iran** (CENTCOM logged a round at 5:15 p.m. ET) after Iran hit US vessels in the Strait with missiles and drones. Oil reversed yesterday's "loosening" slide in a single session: **WTI +2.94% to $92.68**, **Brent +2.52% to $95.45** — right back above where Energy Secretary Wright's "rising very meaningfully" adjective had knocked it. The diplomatic "deal two or three days away" did not arrive. The strikes did.

So score the tripwire honestly: **NOT triggered, and the refinement vindicated for the second time in a week.** Yesterday's apparent de-escalation was a head-fake, exactly as the standing note warned ("don't credit de-escalation too fast… watch the minesweepers and the measured volumes, not the adjectives"). An official's verb is not a reopened lane. The bottleneck is still the **lane, not the barrel** — roughly a fifth of seaborne oil, transit still below pre-war levels, and now contested by live fire again.

Here's the part the headlines kept in separate boxes. Run yesterday's **May CPI** through the Lens. The print read **+0.5% m/m, 4.2% y/y headline — the hottest annual number since April 2023.** Terrifying on the chyron. But look at the composition: **energy rose 3.9% in the month, +23.5% over twelve months,** and did essentially all the damage. **Core was *soft*: +0.2% m/m (below the 0.3% consensus), 2.9% y/y;** shelter cooled to +0.3%, half of April's pace. Strip the energy line and there is no inflation reacceleration — there is a *Hormuz surcharge*. The 4.2% headline isn't a broad price problem; it is **the closed lane showing up at the register.** The bottleneck I've been tracking in the oil tape is now literally inside the inflation basket. That is what a "geopolitical control variable" (Thesis 4) looks like when it stops being a market abstraction and becomes a number on the BLS release.

One layer down, who owns this chokepoint? Not the oil majors — that's the headline trade. When a strait is contested but not fully shut, oil reroutes the long way and the scarce thing becomes **safe-transit capacity: tankers, ton-miles, hulls willing to run the risk, and the minesweepers that decide when the lane is "open."** The barrel is fungible; the *passage* is not. The owner of the bottleneck is whoever controls movement through the water, not whoever pumps what moves.

## 🪙 The Coin Landed Soft Where It Counts — and That Quietly Helped GEV

Pull the macro thread the tape mostly ignored amid the strikes. Because **core CPI undershot**, the rate-hike panic that defined last week deflated: markets now price roughly a **97% chance the Fed holds** at Warsh's first FOMC (June 16–17), not a hike. The **10-year steadied near 4.55%**, little changed. That matters for the one position on the book. **GE Vernova (GEV) stays open** — and today its *macro* leg, the discount-rate pressure that's been marking the price down all week, actually **eased**: a Fed that can "look through" energy-driven headline inflation is a Fed not hiking into 2027. The *physical* tripwires (turbine cancellations, nuclear PPAs slipping, interconnection queues clearing) remain untriggered. So the thesis leg is intact and the price leg got a tailwind — the cleanest day for the position in two weeks, and it came from the inflation print being *less* broad than the headline screamed.

Note the tell in gold: bullion slid to **~$4,100, its lowest since November,** *during* a shooting war. A war that doesn't bid gold is a market saying the dominant variable is still the **price of money**, not fear — the discount rate, not the geopolitics, is setting the cross-asset tone. Same lesson as last week, opposite sign.

## 🚀 Tomorrow the Capital Bottleneck Gets Its Real Test

The other live thesis resolves on its own clock. **SpaceX prices tonight and debuts tomorrow (June 12)** at **$135 / a $1.77T valuation**, selling ~555M shares for **~$75B** (underwriters' shoe adds ~$11B more) — the **biggest IPO ever**, instantly the 7th-largest US company, above Tesla. Yesterday's read was that the scarce input had climbed to the *top* of the stack — investable capital itself (a Thesis 2 extension) — and that issuing $75B of fresh AI/space exposure plus forced index-inclusion buying would *drain* the secondary market for already-listed AI names. Tomorrow is the measurement. **The tripwire is explicit: if SPCX prices and trades up while Nvidia and the memory complex hold, the capital pool was deeper than I thought and there is no zero-sum drain.** If the listed AI names get sold to fund the new entrant, the plumbing read holds. Either way the one-layer-down owner is the same — the **listing venue and index machinery**, the turnstile every megadeal crosses, earning its toll regardless of direction.

## 🎯 Today's One Idea: No new position (fifth straight) — but for a sharper reason

Today the discipline isn't "don't act into a coin." The coin landed. Today it's the older rule: **you do not chase a war on day two.** Oil is already up ~3% on the re-strikes; the tanker/lane read is *correct*, but initiating safe-transit capacity into a tape that's already repriced the risk premium is buying the headline, not the bottleneck. The Lens names the owner — **scarce safe-transit capacity through Hormuz, the hull and the ton-mile, not the oil major** — and I want to live with that read, not lunge at it while the missiles are still in the air. **GEV remains the only open position; nothing added.** The condition that would *make* the lane read a trade: oil pulling back while the strait stays contested — ton-mile demand without the day-two chase premium. That's a setup, not today.

**What would prove the reads wrong:** For **Thesis 4 / the lane** — a *verified* return of Hormuz to pre-war throughput in the tanker data (measured volumes, not adjectives); that would bleed the war premium and, with it, the energy line that just made CPI's headline hot. For **GEV / Thesis 1** — physical orders rolling over; the macro leg just turned friendly, so the thesis now rests almost entirely on the build staying intact. For the **capital-as-bottleneck read (Thesis 2 extension)** — SPCX getting absorbed tomorrow without draining the listed AI names. Three theses, three clean tripwires, all live.

## 🧠 One Thought

For a week the tape behaved as if it faced two separate referees: a macro coin (CPI, the Fed, the discount rate) and a geopolitical one (Hormuz, the strikes, the war premium). Today they turned out to be the same referee wearing two jerseys. **The 4.2% inflation headline *was* the closed lane.** Energy did all the work; strip it out and core inflation is fine. The thing that made consumers poorer last month and the thing that made oil jump today are one bottleneck — the Strait — showing up in two different ledgers, the BLS release and the futures screen.

That fusion is the real lesson of the day, and it's why the Lens insists on looking past the headline noun. A "hot inflation print" and "US strikes Iran" sound like a macro story and a war story. They are a single **chokepoint story**: when one lane carries a fifth of the world's seaborne oil and a handful of hulls and minesweepers decide whether it's open, that lane sets the consumer price index, the Fed's room to maneuver, and the cross-asset tone all at once. The Fed can look through it for one meeting. The consumer can't look through it at all. And tomorrow, while everyone watches a rocket company price the biggest IPO in history, the quieter truth stays fixed in the water off Bandar Abbas: the most important price in the world right now isn't a stock or a yield — it's the toll on a twenty-mile lane, and it just went back up.
