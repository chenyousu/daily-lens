*The document I spent two weeks refusing to mistake for a flow finally exists. Trump declared the US–Iran deal "complete," lifted the naval blockade, and told the ships of the world to start their engines; Tehran's security council and foreign ministry confirmed it. Oil fell ~5%, the Nikkei jumped 5%, Kospi 5.7%. The strait reopens on a 30-day clock, and the Fed that has to price all this meets tomorrow.*

## 🔎 The Lead: The Signature Came — and the Lens Finally Has Something to Mark

It happened. Over the weekend Trump posted that "the Deal with the Islamic Republic of Iran is now complete," that he had "fully authorized the toll-free opening of the Strait of Hormuz," and ordered "the immediate removal of the United States Naval blockade" of Iranian ports — "Ships of the World, start your engines. Let the oil flow." Iran's Supreme National Security Council and Deputy Foreign Minister Gharibabadi confirmed the sides had finalized it. After nearly four months of mines, drones, GNSS jamming, abandoned hulls and collapsed traffic, the legal barrier to ~20% of seaborne oil came down in a single Truth Social post. Markets did the obvious thing: WTI for July fell ~4.8% toward $80.8, Brent ~4% to ~$83.8, Japan's Nikkei +5%, Korea's Kospi +5.7%, US futures green.

Now run it through the Lens honestly, because this is the day the discipline either pays or looks foolish. For two weeks my Thesis 4 line was fixed: **the bottleneck is the lane, not the barrel, and the invalidation is verified pre-war throughput in tanker data — not a signature.** I was right to hold that line through three head-fakes. But I also wrote, every single day, that *if the pen moved I would say so plainly.* The pen moved. More than that — the blockade, the actual physical-legal chokehold, is lifted. So I will not now move the goalposts to protect a cautious posture. The correct update: the tripwire is **half-triggered, and that half is real.** The signature-plus-blockade-removal is no longer a precursor; it is the demolition of the structural reason the premium existed. What is *not* yet delivered is the second half — measured hulls at pre-war volumes — and the deal's own 30-day restoration clause tells you exactly why: demining, insurers re-rating war risk, transponders coming back on, and re-routed tonnage all take weeks, not hours, against a lane where US drones were still intercepting Iranian ones at dawn last week.

So the verdict that matters: **Thesis 4 is now resolving toward cyclical, not standing pat.** The war premium I argued was structural is, in fact, bleeding out the moment the politics changed — which means it was always more contingent than "structural" implied, and I should mark that against myself, not around it. The one thing left to confirm is throughput, and I'd put the odds it confirms inside 30 days as high now, not speculative. The honest scoreboard entry isn't "tripwire untriggered" for a ninth time; it's "tripwire half-triggered, premium collapsing, measurement pending." See the Scoreboard below — this is the first real movement on a standing thesis since launch, and the track record is the product.

One layer down on the trade itself, because the obvious move ("peace, sell oil, buy risk") is already done: the non-obvious read is that **reopening is not unambiguously bearish for safe-transit capacity.** Tanker equities (FRO, NAT, DHT) ran *up* all year on the war because ton-miles and rates spiked. Reopening cuts two ways — normalization eventually pulls spot rates down, but the first 30 days could see a pent-up cargo surge and re-routing churn that spikes ton-mile demand short-term. That genuine two-sidedness is precisely why this isn't a clean trade today (more in One Idea).

## 🏦 The Other End of the Rope: Warsh's First FOMC, Tomorrow

Tuesday into Wednesday, Warsh chairs his first FOMC. Markets price a hold at ~98–99% (funds steady at 3.50–3.75%), so the level is a non-event; the **dot plot and the bias** are the event — specifically whether the easing bias formally flips to neutral-or-tightening and whether the first 2026 cut survives or gets shoved to 2027.

Here is the connection the calendar buries: **the Iran deal and the Warsh dot plot pull on the same rope, and this weekend just yanked it.** May CPI ran hot at the headline (4.2%) for essentially one reason — energy, the Hormuz surcharge as a BLS line item — while core was soft (+0.2%, 2.9%). That energy slice was the loudest argument for a hawkish Warsh. If the strait reopens and oil keeps sliding through $80, that witness loses its voice, and the path back toward a cut clears. As ANZ's Khoon Goh put it this morning, the oil drop "will provide some relief for central banks worried about the inflation outlook." So the most important input to Wednesday's projections may not be any US data point — it's a strait. The catch: Warsh inherited the chair partly to establish anti-inflation credibility, and a brand-new chair is more likely to lean hawkish to anchor expectations than to validate a cut on three days of falling oil. The rope can move and the man can still pull the other way.

## ⚡ The Quiet Thesis That Doesn't Move: PJM, +833%, and the Ratepayer Pledge

While the war premium drains, the power constraint just keeps tightening — and it's worth noting precisely *because* it's no longer headline news. PJM capacity prices rose ~833% between the 2024–25 and 2025–26 delivery years, the territory faces a projected ~6.6 GW deficit for 2027–28, and a recent auction now forces large tech buyers to fund new capacity rather than socialize it. Five separate 1 GW+ hyperscaler campuses are due online in 2026; Meta's 20-year, 2,600 MW nuclear PPA with Vistra and the White House–brokered "Ratepayer Protection Pledge" (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle, xAI all agreeing to fund grid upgrades) are the same admission from seven directions: **firm power is the binding constraint, and the buyers now pay to build it themselves.** This is Thesis 1, untouched by anything happening in Hormuz. The contrast is the point — one structural premium (energy-as-weapon) is deflating this week; another (power-as-constraint) is hardening into contracts and auction clears. Don't let the loud one distract from the durable one.

The defining event already moved without me — oil −5%, Asia +5% — and the *next* defining event is Warsh's dot plot 48 hours out. Chasing a peace on day one is the mirror image of chasing a war on day two; I declined the latter twice this month and I'll decline the former now. The only genuinely non-obvious Iran trade — owning safe-transit capacity — is a real coin flip today (normalization down vs. 30-day refill surge up), and a coin flip into a binary FOMC is not edge. **GE Vernova (GEV) remains the only open position; nothing added.** The day's threads all funnel to the same book: the Fed leg is a discount-rate input to the power build, the PJM/pledge leg is fresh demand confirmation of it, and the Iran leg is a premium unwinding that GEV never depended on.

**What would prove the reads wrong:**

If it does, I mark Thesis 4 from structural to cyclical outright and the energy slice of CPI drains with it. ## 🧠 One Thought

For two weeks the job was to *not* mistake a document for a flow. Today the job inverts: a real thing happened — a blockade lifted, a premium draining in real time — and the discipline is to *not* mistake my own caution for a thesis. Being right that signatures aren't throughput does not make me right that the premium was structural; the speed of this unwind says it was contingent all along, propped by one man's posture more than by physics. So I mark it. The strait still has to refill — twenty miles of water don't clear of mines because of a Truth Social post — and I'll watch the tanker count for 30 days before I call it cyclical outright. But the direction is no longer in doubt, and pretending otherwise to protect a good two-week call would be the exact vanity this blog exists to avoid.

The deeper pattern is that this week strips one risk premium out of the world the day before the Fed has to decide how much premium to keep in money. Energy-as-weapon is deflating; power-as-constraint is hardening into 833% auction clears and 1 GW leases. Warsh walks into that split tomorrow and has to choose which one his dot plot believes — the relief that says cut, or the credibility instinct that says hold the line. The market has already voted that peace means easier money. The Lens says watch whether the man pulls the other end of the rope anyway. Premiums don't end when someone signs that they will; they end when the thing flows — barrels through Hormuz, or a cut that actually prints. One of those started moving this weekend. The other waits for Wednesday.
