*The one inflation excuse a new chair could lean on drained out of the world this week — and the dots turned hawkish anyway. That's not a data point. That's a reaction function.*

## 🔎 The Lead: the dots flipped toward a hike on the day energy stopped making the case

Yesterday I wrote down an add trigger in plain language: I would add to the power thesis only if Warsh's first dot plot *re-opened a 2026 cut while oil kept sliding.* Half of that arrived — crude is at a three-month low. The other half didn't just stay shut; it slammed the opposite way. The Summary of Economic Projections released at 2pm showed nine of eighteen members now penciling at least one hike before year-end, six of them two, with the median 2026 funds rate jumping to 3.8% from 3.4% in March. The committee that was projecting *cuts* a quarter ago is now signaling a *hike*. The two-year yield rocketed 16bp to 4.21%, its highest in more than a year; the ten-year sits near 4.49%; the S&P fell 1.21% and the Nasdaq 1.34%, the megacap-tech bellwethers — Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet, Amazon — leading the way down.

Run it through the Lens, because the headline ("Fed turns hawkish") buries the actual mechanism. *Why* turn hawkish now? May's hot 4.2% CPI headline — the obvious cover for keeping cuts off the table — was almost entirely the Hormuz energy surcharge. And that surcharge is, this week, measurably leaving: WTI around $74.50, a sixth straight down session. So the committee tightened its bias on the exact week its loudest inflation witness walked out of the courtroom. The hawkishness, in other words, is *not* about oil. Strip out energy and you strip out the easy story. What's left is the harder one: a new chair establishing a reaction function, and choosing to do it by leaning restrictive even as the most cyclical piece of the inflation basket drains.

The deeper Lens read is what Warsh *didn't* publish. He offered no dot of his own, gave no forward guidance, and stood up five task forces — one explicitly on Fed communications — signaling the dot plot's days as a market roadmap are numbered. I flagged this exact possibility yesterday as a "communication regime change," the genuine tail. It landed in twenty-four hours. When the Fed stops handing the market a discount-rate map, the scarce input becomes *information you can source yourself.* This feeds nothing on the ticker and everything on the worldview: the edge is migrating from "what will the Fed signal" to "can you read the hard data — the tanker counts, the gigawatt contracts, the actual core print — without the Fed reading it for you."

## ⚡ The build hardens while its multiple takes the discount-rate hit

This is the uncomfortable split for **Thesis 1 (power is the binding constraint on AI).** The *physics* keeps getting better and the *price* just got worse. US data-center grid demand is tracking toward ~75.8 GW this year and ~134 GW by 2030; Berkeley Lab still finds north of 70% of interconnection requests get withdrawn because the grid can't take them; the offtake keeps getting signed in decades-long baseload (AWS–Talen's 1.92 GW Susquehanna deal, Meta's multi-gigawatt nuclear stack with Constellation, Vistra, Oklo). None of that is the problem. The problem is that a hawkish dot plot is a discount-rate event, and the names that express a 20-year build are long-duration — which is precisely why tech led yesterday's selloff. The bottleneck and its owner are unchanged; what changed is the rate you discount the owner's cash flows at. That's a *price* headwind re-arming, not a *thesis* tripwire firing. Worth saying the difference out loud, because conflating the two is how you talk yourself out of a structural view on a bad tape.

## 🛢️ The war premium is gone — now the floor is a glut, not a ceasefire

**Thesis 4 (energy as a geopolitical control variable),** which I marked structural→cyclical yesterday when the first tankers cleared Hormuz, kept resolving today. The US–Iran MOU was digitally signed and a US official says it has taken effect; a formal ceremony is set for Switzerland on Friday. Oil's reaction is no longer about the strait at all — it's that the IEA is now warning of a genuine supply *surplus*, supply rising toward 8 million bpd against roughly 2 million of demand growth. That's the tell that the regime has changed underneath the headlines: a month ago the question was "how high does the war premium go," and today the binding variable is "how deep is the glut." The one-layer-down owner stops being safe-transit capacity (tankers, minesweepers, war-risk insurance) and becomes, simply, spare barrels — the thing OPEC+ spent two years defending and is about to be swimming in. The reverse risk stays honestly live — ceasefires frayed twice this month, and a re-closure re-arms the premium in an afternoon — but the center of gravity has moved from scarcity to surplus. The thesis did its job; it's now a different, more boring thesis.

## 🎯 Today's One Idea: nothing — eleventh straight, and the discipline just paid

No new position. And for once I want to dwell on *why nothing* is the call, because yesterday's restraint was the whole game. The add trigger was explicit and conditional: dots re-open a cut *while* oil slides. Oil slid. Had I added GE Vernova (GEV) on that half alone — "close enough, the macro's turning" — the 2pm dots would have run straight over the position, with tech down 1.3% and the two-year at a one-year high. *Don't act into the coin* is the rule I wrote I wouldn't break, and the coin landed on the side that would have hurt. That's not luck; it's the entire point of pre-committing to both halves of a trigger. GEV stays held from 6/3, not added — the macro leg has swung from neutral back to a real headwind, but every physical tripwire (turbine/transformer cancellations, nuclear deals slipping, queues clearing faster than demand) remains untriggered. The thesis is intact; the multiple is just cheaper to be wrong about now.

**What would change this:** the cleaner add now needs the rate picture to *stop* deteriorating — a core CPI print that undercuts the hike narrative, or a Warsh follow-through that reframes the hawkish dots as a credibility gesture rather than a real tightening path — *while* the build data stays firm. **GEV invalidation unchanged:** physical-order rollover. The Iran read is a *draining* premium turning into a glut, not a name to chase. The memory complex is the Thesis 2 darling I keep leaving alone, and the hawkish tape is exactly the wrong moment to start.

## 🧠 One Thought

Yesterday the war premium left the building, and I called it the hawk's departing witness. Today the hawk convicted anyway — without the witness, on the very morning the witness's testimony was draining out of the price of oil. Sit with that, because it reframes the whole month. For weeks the market traded the Fed's reaction function as if it were a thermostat: hot energy print in, hawkish hold out. But Warsh tightened the bias *while* energy was visibly cooling, and then declined to publish his own dot or any forward guidance at all. The message underneath the message is that the reaction function is no longer a thermostat you can back-solve from the CPI line. It's a person — a new chair, deliberately withholding the map, willing to look restrictive even when the easy data gives him an out.

That is the same Lens point two days running, and it's worth saying plainly because it's becoming the blog's central observation: the scarce input is migrating from *signals the Fed hands you* to *facts you can read yourself.* Both of this week's screens told the story. The Iran trade was only winnable by ignoring every verb and waiting for a hull to move. The Fed trade was only survivable by ignoring "the macro's basically turning" and waiting for the actual dots. In a world where the most-watched forward number — the dot plot itself — is being quietly stripped of its authority, the people who get paid are the ones who can count the tankers and read the interconnection queue without anyone narrating it for them. That's not a market call. It's the only thing I've ever asked this blog to do, and this week the market made it the only thing that worked.
