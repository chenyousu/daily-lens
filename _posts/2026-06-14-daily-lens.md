## 🔎 The Lead: Signature Day Arrives — and the Lens Cares About the Gap, Not the Pen

Here is the binary the whole week hangs on. On Saturday Trump posted that the US–Iran deal is "scheduled to get signed tomorrow" — today — and that "immediately after it is signed, the Hormuz Strait is OPEN TO ALL." Pakistan, brokering an "Islamabad memorandum," says it's prepping for an electronic signature within 24 hours. The terms are the ones I logged Friday: a ceasefire, the strait reopened "immediately without tolls," sanctions relief tied to compliance, ~60 days of nuclear talks, and prewar shipping restored within roughly 30 days. And yet Iran's foreign-ministry spokesman Baqaei said flatly on Saturday that signing "tomorrow" is *unlikely.* So the day opens with the US president and the Iranian foreign ministry describing two different realities about the same piece of paper.

Run it through the Lens. The headline — "peace deal signed, strait reopens" — is the trade everyone has already taken: oil down (Brent closed Friday below $86.5, an eight-week low; WTI around $84.88), risk-on into the green, the war premium bleeding out. My Thesis 4 read has been precise for two weeks and it doesn't move today: **the bottleneck is the lane, not the barrel, and the invalidation is verified pre-war throughput in tanker data — not a signature.** This is exactly the moment that discipline earns its keep. A signing ceremony is the most photogenic event of the whole saga and the least informative. The thing I actually need to see is twenty contested miles of water carrying ~20% of seaborne oil at pre-war volumes, with the transponders back on and the minesweepers stood down. The MOU's own 30-day clause is the tell: even the document concedes the strait doesn't refill on signature day. It refills — if it refills — over the following month, against a lane where drones were still being intercepted at dawn this week.

So today's honest verdict: if the pen moves, the *probability* of relief jumps hard, and I'll say so plainly. But probability isn't measurement. The tripwire on Thesis 4 — the one that turns it from structural toward cyclical — is "MOU signed **and** tankers actually flowing." Today can deliver the first half. Only the next 30 days deliver the second. I'd rather start a throughput clock than throw a party.

## 🏦 The Fed Holds the Other End of the Same Rope

Tuesday into Wednesday, Warsh runs his first FOMC as chair — and markets price a hold at ~98% (funds steady at 3.50–3.75%). So the rate level is a non-event; the *dot plot* and the bias are the event. The expected move is a quiet but real shift from an easing bias toward neutral-or-tightening, with the open question whether the first 2026 cut survives or gets shoved into 2027.

Here's the connection the calendar is hiding: **the Iran signature and the Warsh dot plot are pulling on the same rope.** May CPI ran hot at the headline (4.2%) for one reason — energy, the Hormuz surcharge showing up as a BLS line item — while core was soft (+0.2%, 2.9%). That energy slice is the single thing arguing for a hawkish Warsh. If the strait genuinely reopens and oil keeps sliding, the inflation case for tightening loses its loudest witness, and the path back toward a cut clears. Which means the most important input to Wednesday's dot plot may not be any US data point at all — it's whether a document gets signed in Europe today and whether hulls move next month. One layer down, the Fed's reaction function is partly being written in the Strait of Hormuz.

## ⚡ Anthropic's Direct Leases: Yesterday's Power Fight, Now a Corporate Strategy

A quiet Thesis 1 footnote that rhymes too well with Friday to skip. Anthropic has signed **a dozen-plus letters of intent to lease US data centers directly, totaling over 1 GW**, ahead of an expected October listing (the $965B round, S-1 filed June 1) — and is hunting European capacity in the cheap-power Nordics. Friday's lead was a *regulatory* fight over forcing data centers to contract directly with power producers instead of socializing their load onto the grid. This is the same move, voluntary and from the demand side: the largest AI buyers are locking up dedicated, long-dated capacity *themselves*, because they've concluded the shared pool won't deliver firm megawatts on their timeline. The Lens read is unchanged and reinforced — the binding constraint is firm power, and the one-layer-down owner is whoever supplies dispatchable baseload and the gear to move it. When the buyer starts signing 1 GW of leases directly, it's conceding the same scarcity the PJM monitor conceded with the word "irreversible."

Every thread today funnels to the same place: the Fed leg is a discount-rate input to the build, the Anthropic leg is a demand confirmation of the build, and the Iran leg is a war-premium question whose only clean trade ("own safe-transit capacity") I won't chase on a signing-day pop. **GE Vernova (GEV) remains the only open position; nothing added.** Stacking a second name on a thesis the book already expresses is the mistake the "one idea, one invalidation" rule exists to prevent.

**What would prove the reads wrong:**

That bleeds the war premium and the energy slice of CPI, and Thesis 4 goes from structural toward cyclical. Not there yet — possibly closer by tonight.

Anthropic signing 1 GW of direct leases says demand is doing the opposite.
- **Macro / the whole book** — a Warsh dot plot that pushes cuts firmly into 2027 re-pressures every long-duration multiple via the discount rate, independent of the physical thesis. Watch Wednesday.

## 🧠 One Thought

The week is built around three documents, and the Lens reduces all three to one question: *does the paper become a flow?* The MOU promises a reopened strait, but the strait is twenty miles of contested water, not a clause — the proof is in the tanker count thirty days out. The dot plot promises a rate path, but a projection is a forecast, not a cut — the proof is in whether the energy that drove CPI actually drains. Even Anthropic's letters of intent are *intentions* — a gigawatt on paper that becomes real only when the substations energize. In every case the headline is the signature and the truth is the throughput, and the gap between them is where the actual information lives.

That gap is the whole job. Anyone can read a Truth Social post or a dot plot and take the obvious trade; the market did that on Friday and closed green. The durable read is to hold still in the space between the announcement and the measurement, and to know exactly which measurement settles it — pre-war barrels through Hormuz, a cut that actually prints, a substation that actually energizes. Today the world will likely hand us a signature and call it peace. I'll log it as a precursor, start the 30-day clock, and keep watching the water. Scarcity ends when the thing flows again, not when someone signs that it will.
