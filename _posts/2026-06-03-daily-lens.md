## 🔥 Top Story: The Rally Finally Blinks

**The Headline:** After a nine-day melt-up that printed the S&P 500's first-ever close above 7,600, the bulls ran out of runway. The S&P slid 0.74% to roughly 7,560, the Nasdaq dropped 0.89%, and the Dow took the worst of it — down 620.72 points, or 1.21%, to about 50,859.

**The Move:** This was a textbook risk-off rotation, not a crash. Money fled the most crowded trades — megacap chips and momentum tech — and parked in the usual fear hedges as oil climbed back toward triple digits and the 10-year Treasury yield pushed back near 4.5%. When the cost of money rises and the price of oil rises at the same time, the discounted-cash-flow math under every high-multiple stock gets uglier in a hurry.

**The Context:** Three forces hit at once: renewed US-Iran missile exchanges, a hawkish repricing of the Fed (markets now lean toward a hike before year-end, not a cut), and a pair of marquee chip and cybersecurity earnings that landed after the bell and gave nervous traders an excuse to sell first and read the footnotes later.

**Why It Matters:** A nine-day streak ending on a 620-point Dow day is the market reminding everyone that "priced for perfection" cuts both ways. Breadth had been narrowing for a week — the index was being carried by a handful of AI names while the average stock did nothing. When the generals retreat, there's no infantry to hold the line.

**Tickers:** SPY, DIA, QQQ, ^VIX, AVGO, CRWD

## 💾 Broadcom and CrowdStrike: Great Numbers, Ugly Tape

**The Headline:** Both AI-darling chips-and-security names reported strong fundamentals and both got punished in after-hours trading — Broadcom down roughly 13%, CrowdStrike off about 10%.

**The Numbers:** Broadcom posted record revenue, operating profit, and free cash flow on accelerating AI semiconductor demand, and guided Q3 revenue to roughly $29.4 billion with eye-watering ~67% non-GAAP operating margins. CrowdStrike beat with EPS of $1.10 against $0.88 expected, logged record Q1 net-new ARR of $256 million (up 32% YoY), generated $468 million in free cash flow, and announced a four-for-one stock split.

**The Reaction:** So why the bloodbath? Because when a stock is up 200% and trades at 40x sales, "great" isn't good enough — only "flawless plus a raised outlook" clears the bar. Broadcom's top line nicked the highest whisper numbers; CrowdStrike's forward guide read merely solid rather than spectacular. In a tape already spooked by oil and yields, that's all the algos needed.

**Why It Matters:** This is the AI trade's first real stress test of the summer. The businesses are genuinely excellent — but valuations had front-run the fundamentals so far that even record quarters can't levitate the shares. Watch whether dip-buyers show up tomorrow; their absence would say more than any earnings line.

**Tickers:** AVGO, CRWD, SMH, PANW, NVDA

## 🤖 Nvidia Goes All-In: $100B for OpenAI, a New CPU, and a Robot

**The Headline:** While chips sold off, Nvidia spent the week stacking the deck for the next leg of the AI build-out — and the ambitions are staggering.

**The Deals:** Nvidia and OpenAI signed a letter of intent to deploy at least 10 gigawatts of Nvidia systems, with Nvidia intending to invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI progressively as each gigawatt comes online. The first gigawatt lands in the second half of 2026 on the new Vera Rubin platform. At Computex, Nvidia also unveiled the RTX Spark "superchip" for AI PCs (shipping this fall from Dell, HP, Lenovo, ASUS, MSI, and Microsoft Surface), put its Vera data-center CPUs into full production with Anthropic, OpenAI, and SpaceX as early customers, and showed off Isaac GR00T, a nearly-six-foot humanoid robot with dexterous five-fingered hands.

**Why It Matters:** Nvidia is no longer just selling shovels in the gold rush — it's financing the miners, building the town, and now manufacturing the robots. A $100B circular bet on its largest customer is brilliant if compute demand keeps compounding and a vendor-financing house of cards if it doesn't. Either way, it deepens the AI complex's reflexivity: Nvidia funds OpenAI, OpenAI buys Nvidia, the revenue books, the stock rises, repeat.

**Tickers:** NVDA, MSFT, DELL, HPQ, AMD

## 🛡️ Iran War: Missiles Miss, and Congress Pushes Back

**The Headline:** CENTCOM reported that a fresh barrage of Iranian missiles and drones aimed at Gulf neighbors largely failed — three missiles fired at Bahrain were intercepted, two aimed at Kuwait fell short or broke apart, and a drone wave on US forces in Kuwait was knocked down without casualties.

**The Politics:** On the same day, the US House approved a war-powers resolution to halt military action against Iran — a striking bipartisan rebuke aimed at reining in the executive. Operation Epic Fury, the joint US-Israel campaign launched February 28, officially wound down May 5, but the tit-for-tat missile exchanges show the embers are still hot.

**Why It Matters:** Markets care about one thing here: the Strait of Hormuz. Shipping is still rerouting, flights are still disrupted, and every failed-but-fired missile keeps a war premium baked into crude. The House vote injects a new variable — domestic political will to de-escalate — which is bullish for risk if it holds and noise if the White House ignores it.

**Tickers:** USO, BNO, XOM, LMT, RTX

## 🌍 Ukraine Strikes St. Petersburg as Putin Hosts the Money

**The Headline:** Ukrainian drones struck and set ablaze an oil terminal in St. Petersburg, President Zelenskyy said — pointedly, while the city was hosting Putin's flagship annual economic forum.

**The Context:** The symbolism is the strategy. Hitting energy infrastructure in Russia's second city during a showcase event designed to project stability and woo investors is psychological warfare as much as it is a strike on throughput. It also keeps a second front of geopolitical risk live in global oil markets, layered right on top of the Gulf.

**Why It Matters:** Two simultaneous energy-war theaters — the Gulf and Russia — is exactly the cocktail that keeps crude bid and central bankers hawkish. For anyone wondering why oil won't quit, this is half the answer.

**Tickers:** USO, BNO, RSX, CVX

## 🎬 Culture: Mario Eyes a Billion, and a Voice Goes Quiet

**The Headline:** "The Super Mario Galaxy Movie" sits at roughly $991.8 million worldwide — about $8 million from becoming 2026's first billion-dollar film, likely by this weekend. Meanwhile the music world lost a giant: two-time Grammy winner Peabo Bryson died June 2 at age 75 following a stroke.

**The Context:** Animation and IP nostalgia continue to be the most bankable bet in Hollywood, even as live-action mid-budget films struggle. On streaming, Netflix's "Michael Jackson: The Verdict" docuseries debuted today, reopening one of pop culture's most litigated debates just as the long-gestating "Michael" biopic chases a record music-biopic opening.

**Why It Matters:** The attention economy keeps consolidating around franchises and familiar names — a theme that rhymes with the market's own narrowing breadth. Whether it's box office or index points, 2026 keeps rewarding the few at the top.

**Tickers:** NFLX, CMCSA, DIS, SPOT

## 🧠 One Thought

For nine sessions the market told itself a tidy story: AI demand is infinite, the Fed is friendly, and geopolitics is background noise. Today all three legs wobbled at once. Broadcom and CrowdStrike proved that even excellent results can't outrun valuations that have already priced in the next two years. Oil and yields reminded everyone that the macro backdrop is tightening, not loosening. And a fresh round of missiles — plus a House vote to stop the war — showed the Iran situation is anything but resolved.

The through-line is concentration. A handful of names carried the indices up, a handful dragged them down, and a handful of franchises own the box office. Concentration feels like strength on the way up and fragility on the way down — and tonight, for the first time in a while, it felt like the latter. The bet for tomorrow isn't whether the AI story is real (it is); it's whether the price you're paying for it leaves any margin for the world to disappoint. Today, it didn't.
