*A standing thesis half-resolves on the same morning a new Fed chair has to draw his first dot — and for once the news isn't a signature, it's a measurement.*

## 🔎 The Lead: the tankers moved, and they took the hawks' witness with them

For two months I've written the same line about the Strait of Hormuz: ignore the verbs, ignore the documents, watch the hulls. The invalidation on **Thesis 4 (energy as a geopolitical control variable)** was never a signed MOU or a presidential post — it was *verified pre-war throughput in the tanker data*. This morning the hulls moved. Tanker trackers confirmed Iran's first crude export in two months clearing the strait — at least two NITC supertankers, *Diona* and *Hero 2*, passing through where the US naval blockade stood a week ago. Traffic had been running roughly 95% below normal. A formal US–Iran signing is slated for Switzerland on Friday.

So run it through the Lens one more time. The bottleneck all month was the *lane* — safe-transit capacity, war-risk insurance, demining — not the barrel. The lane is now, measurably, opening. WTI is around $76 and Brent near $83, the fifth straight down session and the lowest since early March; something like $15–17 a barrel of war premium has bled out since the off-ramp firmed. This is the half that was missing yesterday, when I wrote "the pen moved, the hulls haven't." Today the hulls did. The tripwire I've carried for a week — *MOU signed AND tankers flowing* — now has both halves true.

I am marking **Thesis 4 from structural toward cyclical.** Honestly, and with the caveat that matters: two supertankers is the *start* of measured flow, not a return to pre-war volume, and the deal's own text concedes ~30 days to restore prewar shipping. The premium is collapsing faster than the physical flow is rebuilding, which is exactly why the reverse risk stays live — ceasefires frayed twice this month, and a re-closure re-arms everything in an afternoon. But the direction is no longer in doubt, and pretending otherwise to protect a thesis would be the opposite of the job. The energy line in CPI is going to drain with this.

Which is what makes the timing almost too neat. Warsh draws his first dot plot at 2pm ET today (hold at 3.50–3.75% priced near 97%, a fourth straight). May CPI's hot 4.2% headline — the cover a reputationally-hawkish new chair would want to keep cuts off the table — was almost entirely the Hormuz surcharge. That witness didn't just lose its voice; this morning it left the building. The Fed's reaction function has spent a month being co-written by minesweepers. Today the minesweepers filed their last brief before the dots.

## 📊 Scoreboard: Thesis 4 marked structural → cyclical (partial)

First real movement on a standing thesis since tracking began, logged honestly. The *discipline* — "hold for a measurement, not a signature" — was vindicated through three head-fakes this month (Wright's "rising meaningfully" 6/10, the strike reversal 6/11, the document-not-flow stretch 6/12–14). The *framing* takes a partial miss: when politics flipped on 6/15, ~$15/bbl drained in hours, so "structural" oversold the durability — it was more contingent on posture than the thesis claimed. Status: signature + blockade removal + first measured flow all done; pre-war throughput still pending the 30-day clock. If tanker data confirms pre-war volumes inside that window, I mark T4 structural→cyclical **outright** and the energy slice of CPI goes with it. The physical theses (power, memory, magnets) are untouched by this.

## 🟨 The dot plot: watch the regime, not the median

The settled part is the rate. The live part is what Warsh does with the dots themselves. He's questioned the usefulness of the dot plot publicly for years, and the genuine tail today isn't a hawkish median — it's a *communication regime change*: a chair who downgrades, reframes, or withholds forward guidance entirely. That would be the real signal, and it's a Lens story in its own right. When the Fed stops handing the market a discount-rate roadmap, the scarce input becomes *information* — and the premium shifts onto whoever can read the hard data without the Fed's help. The tell I flagged yesterday stands: watch the *dispersion* of the dots (reporting suggests a deeply split committee — multiple dissents, a bloc seeing no 2026 cut at all), not the headline number. A new chair leaning hawkish for **credibility** regardless of where oil sits remains the obvious risk to the "disinflation clears the path" read.

## ⚡ The build keeps hardening underneath all of it

While the war premium drains, the build premium does the opposite — quietly, on a separate clock. US data-center electricity demand has gone from ~23 GW in 2023 to ~42 GW now, and the contracts keep getting signed in baseload: Talen expanding Amazon's Susquehanna nuclear supply to ~1,920 MW through 2042, Meta and Microsoft locking 20-year nuclear deals with Constellation. This is **Thesis 1 (power is the binding constraint on AI)** doing what it does — not making headlines, just turning into 20-year offtake agreements. The one-layer-down owner is unchanged: firm power and the people who can deliver it on a grid that can't add capacity fast enough. As yields ease and the war premium leaves, the discount-rate headwind that marked these names down all month is the lightest it's been in weeks. The thesis now rests almost entirely on the build staying intact — and the build is signing decades-long contracts, not canceling them.

## 🧠 One Thought

Today is the day the blog's two opposite-pointing screens both moved a frame — and they moved *toward* each other. The war premium, which I've tracked as a structural feature of the world for a month, turned out to be more contingent than I framed it: two tankers and a signature drained $15 a barrel, and I'm marking that miss. The build premium did the reverse — no drama, just another 20-year nuclear contract, the scarce input getting more locked-in, not less. One premium proved softer than its thesis; the other keeps proving harder.

The deeper thing is *which kind of fact moved the needle.* All month the noise was verbs and documents — "rising meaningfully," "deal in two days," "scheduled to sign tomorrow" — and every time the discipline was to wait for a number. This morning a number finally arrived: a ship's transponder, lit, in the strait. That's the whole Lens in one image. The market spent a month trading the adjectives; the thesis only moved when a hull did. And it lands on the exact afternoon a new chair may decide to give the market *fewer* numbers to trade on — to pull the dot plot, the most-watched forward number there is, off the table. If he does, the edge stops being "what will the Fed signal" and becomes "can you read the tanker data and the gigawatt contracts yourself." That's a world that rewards looking at the hulls. It's the only thing I've ever asked this blog to do.
