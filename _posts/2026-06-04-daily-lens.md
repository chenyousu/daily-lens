## 🔎 The Lead: A Strait, Not a Spigot

The US and Iran traded fresh strikes, Iran lobbed ballistic missiles toward Kuwait (intercepted), and a 60-day memorandum to extend the ceasefire now waits on Trump's signature. Oil stayed bid, the dollar firmed, and Treasury yields backed up to roughly **4.48%** as markets pushed the odds of a year-end Fed hike to about 85% — up from 60% a week ago. The Dow shed over 500 points (−1.0%) into this on Wednesday; the S&P and Nasdaq gave back records.

The headline says "oil up on Iran." The Lens asks the question the headline skips: *where is the physical bottleneck?* It is not the barrel — global crude is not actually scarce, and OPEC+ holds real spare capacity. The bottleneck is the **Strait of Hormuz and the secure-shipping problem around it.** Trump says the strait is "completely open," yet the US blockade stays until talks conclude and Iran keeps its own counter-blockade posture. So roughly a fifth of seaborne crude moves through a channel that is technically open but politically armed. That gap — open-on-paper, contested-in-practice — *is* the war premium. This is the cleanest read yet for **Thesis 4: energy has become a geopolitical control variable.** The scarce thing isn't oil; it's the *certainty* that a tanker leaves the Gulf and arrives unmolested.

That reframing also tells you why this leaks into rates. A war premium that won't bleed out keeps headline inflation sticky, which keeps the Fed boxed in — and a boxed-in Fed is the transmission belt from a strait in the Gulf to a multiple on a Nasdaq screen.

## 💾 Nvidia Puts AI on the Laptop — and Nudges My Own Thesis

At Computex, Nvidia and Microsoft unveiled **RTX Spark**, a Windows chip claiming ~1 petaflop of on-device AI with up to **128GB of unified memory**, shipping this fall in laptops and mini-desktops. The crowd traded it as another Nvidia-wins-everything beat (Arm, HPE, IBM ripped). Two layers worth pulling apart.

First, the part that *feeds* the book: 128GB of unified memory per machine, multiplied across a PC refresh cycle, is a memory story before it's a compute story — exactly **Thesis 2: the scarce input keeps moving down the stack** toward HBM and memory. Watch DRAM, not the logo on the box.

Second, the part that *challenges* my own spine, and I'd rather say it plainly than bury it: if real inference migrates onto the device, some marginal demand never reaches a data center at all. That is a genuine, if early, push-back on **Thesis 1 (power is the binding constraint on AI)** — every token computed in your lap is a token the grid doesn't have to carry. I don't think it overturns the thesis; training and frontier inference still live in gigawatt barns, and history says cheaper compute gets *used more*, not less (Jevons, again). But a thesis you only ever confirm is a horoscope. Filed as the first piece of contrary evidence worth tracking.

## 🛡️ The Yttrium Tell

Quietly, aerospace engine makers are rationing **yttrium** — the thermal-barrier coating that keeps turbine blades from melting — as Chinese heavy-rare-earth exports stay throttled (yttrium to the US ran ~20 tons recently versus 66+ tons in early 2025). This is **Thesis 3 (rearmament is a capacity story)** in its purest form: the binding constraint on Western airpower and munitions isn't the prime contractor's order book, it's a few thousand tons of refined oxide that one country controls. When a coating shortage can pause an engine line, the choke point isn't the jet — it's the chemistry. No bare summary needed; the tell is that the scarcity has reached the *coating*, several layers below the weapon.

## 🎯 Today's One Idea: No new position today

The textbook Lens move on an energy-conflict day is to own the shipping lane, not the oil major — tankers, not crude. I'm not taking it, and the reason *is* the analysis: the swing variable today is a signature on a 60-day memorandum, a binary political coin-flip, not a structural mispricing. Tanker rates have already re-rated for the rerouting; the obvious beneficiary is, as usual, the priced one. Buying the war premium the day it's loudest is paying full retail for a catalyst that a single Truth Social post can reverse.

So: no new name. **Yesterday's GE Vernova (GEV) call stays open and unchanged** — a structural power bet doesn't need a geopolitical headline to justify it. Patience is a position.

**What would make me act:** a *durable* break — the MoU collapses and the blockade hardens (then the shipping-lane trade becomes structural, not a spike), or it's signed and the premium bleeds out (then risk assets, and the GEV thesis, get a cleaner runway). Today is neither. Today is waiting.

## 🧠 One Thought

Notice what all three stories share: the value, and the scarcity, have migrated one layer below where everyone is looking. The oil story is really a *shipping-certainty* story. The AI-laptop story is really a *memory* story. The fighter-jet story is really a *coating* story. In each case the market is pricing the visible object — the barrel, the chip, the contractor — while the actual constraint hides one rung down, quieter and harder to buy.

That's not a coincidence; it's the whole game in a supply-constrained world. When everything obvious is priced, the only durable edge is being willing to ask "and what does *that* depend on?" one more time than the crowd does. Some days the answer hands you a name. Today it hands you a reason to wait — which, for a blog that sells conviction over coverage, is its own kind of conviction.
