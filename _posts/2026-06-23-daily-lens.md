*Two constraints moved in opposite directions today: the energy premium kept bleeding out of oil, while the AI frontier quietly revealed its real chokepoint — a few dozen researchers who can resign by tweet.*

## 🔎 The Lead: Google's worst day in a year is a story about a bottleneck you can't pour concrete around

Alphabet fell ~7% Monday — its sharpest drop in a year, roughly $250B of market cap gone — after two landmark researchers left in a single week. Noam Shazeer, co-author of the 2017 "Attention Is All You Need" paper that *is* the modern transformer, left for OpenAI; John Jumper, the Nobel laureate behind AlphaFold, posted that after nine years he was leaving DeepMind for Anthropic. Google paid $2.7B to bring Shazeer back in 2024. He walked anyway.

The headline read is "Google is losing the AI race." The Lens read is narrower and more useful: **for this story, the binding constraint on frontier capability is not chips and not power — it is the tiny, mobile pool of people who can actually push the frontier.** And that input has a property none of our physical bottlenecks share. You can build a fab in three years, a gas turbine plant in two, a transformer with a long-enough queue. You cannot manufacture a Shazeer, and the one you have can email a resignation on a Tuesday. The scarce input here doesn't sit in a chokepoint you can own — it owns itself, and it's liquid.

That matters for our standing theses, so let me be precise rather than dramatic. **Thesis 2 says the scarce input keeps moving down the stack** — power → memory → optical interconnect, always toward whatever is *physically* scarce next. This week the migration jumped sideways and *up*, off the physical stack entirely, to human capital. It's the same shape (find what's scarce, ignore the priced-in darling) with a crucial difference: physical chokepoints can be *owned* — you can buy the indium refiner, the memory maker, the turbine supplier. The talent chokepoint can't be owned, only rented, and the rent reprices the instant someone better-funded calls. So this is a refinement, not a contradiction: there are now **two kinds of scarce input** — the ownable physical kind (still the tradeable thesis) and the un-ownable human kind (a real constraint, but not an asset). Google's $250B lesson is what happens when you mistake the second for something you'd already bought.

Does it dent **Thesis 1 (power is the binding constraint on AI)**? No — and the distinction is the whole point. Talent is the constraint on *frontier capability*; power is still the constraint on *deploying compute at scale*. Anthropic and OpenAI can hire every author of the transformer paper and still not run the models without gigawatts. Different bottleneck, different layer, both real. The physical theses are untouched.

## 🤝 Iran talks move to Washington, and oil keeps not caring

The US–Iran negotiations shifted from Switzerland toward a Washington round (reported June 23–25); VP Vance called day one "very, very good" and said Iran would let nuclear inspectors back in, with a Hormuz "deconfliction cell" to keep transit safe. Oil's response: WTI ~$74.2, Brent ~$77.8, both lower again. This is the same tape we've watched all month — escalation paragraphs and de-escalation paragraphs alike, and crude slides regardless.

The Lens read hasn't changed and that *is* the read: **Thesis 4 is now cyclical, not structural.** When the world is structurally long oil (IEA surplus ~8M bpd of supply vs ~2M demand growth, inventories stacking), the lane stops being the binding variable — the glut is. The one-layer-down owner is no longer safe-transit capacity (tankers, minesweepers, war-risk insurance); it's OPEC+ idle barrels and onshore storage. The honest caveat stands: a *sustained* inventory-draining closure — months, not a Friday — would re-arm scarcity the physical way. A diplomatic round in Washington isn't that. The premium keeps draining toward the floor the surplus sets.

## 🏦 The macro frame: Warsh's Fed is the gravity on every multiple

Background, but load-bearing. The Fed held at 3.5–3.75% last week; the debut dot plot leaned hawkish (nine officials see at least one hike, 2026 median nudged to 3.8%, PCE forecast to 3.6%), and BofA now models *three* hikes to 4.25–4.5% this year. May PCE lands Thursday. The relevance to today's lead: a rising discount rate compresses exactly the long-duration AI multiples that the talent panic is already hitting. Alphabet didn't fall in a vacuum — it fell into a tape where the cost of owning a far-off cash flow is going *up*. Two headwinds, one direction.

## 🎯 Today's One Idea

**No new position today.** Fifteenth straight — and today the reason is almost a definition. The cleanest insight of the day (the AI frontier's scarce input is *people*) is precisely the one insight that doesn't resolve to an ownable name: you can't buy the talent, and shorting the company that lost it is chasing a 7% gap into a hawkish-Fed multiple compression with no margin of safety. The physical reads (power, memory, magnets, indium) are intact but already expressed and already richly priced. When the scarce input walks out the door, there's no turnstile to own — so I'll own nothing.

**What would make me act:** a rate picture that *stops* deteriorating (a soft core PCE Thursday) landing alongside a physical-bottleneck name that's been sold off with the broad AI tape rather than bid up with it — scarce input, ownable form, margin of safety, all three at once. None of those three are true today.

## 🧠 One Thought

Two of the world's big constraints moved today, and they moved in opposite directions for the same underlying reason: **abundance defeats a constraint, scarcity defines one.** Oil's war premium keeps bleeding out because the world is structurally *long* barrels — pour enough supply on a chokepoint and even closing 20% of seaborne crude buys you four dollars and a shrug. AI's frontier constraint, meanwhile, got *sharper* this week, because the input that turned out to be binding — a few dozen people — is the one input no amount of capital can mass-produce. Google spent the decade building the foundation of modern AI and then watched the foundation walk to Anthropic and OpenAI.

The lesson the Lens keeps teaching is to ask *what's actually scarce and who owns it.* The unsettling refinement today is that the most scarce input in the most important industry on earth is the one nobody can own — not Google with $2.7B, not anyone. Power you can build. Memory you can fab. Barrels you can pump. People you can only persuade, and persuasion has a market price that just repriced violently. That's not a trade. It's the reason the trades are where they are: the world keeps routing its capital toward the chokepoints it *can* buy, precisely because the one that matters most isn't for sale.
