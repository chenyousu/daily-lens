*Last night's number answered the question this whole week was really asking — the AI unwind was a price, not a verdict — and it turns the standing power position from "hold" to one print away from "add."*

## 🔎 The Lead: Micron didn't beat the quarter, it extended the shortage

I spent yesterday's post insisting two days of semiconductor carnage were a verdict on a multiple, not on demand, and that the answer would come from Micron's allocation book rather than the tape. The book opened last night, and it is not close.

The headline beat is almost beside the point: Micron guided next quarter to $49–51B in revenue against a ~$43B consensus and $30–32 in EPS against ~$25, and JPMorgan responded by lifting its target from $550 to $1,540. The stock closed $1,048.51 and traded up roughly 18% premarket toward ~$1,214; Qualcomm tacked on ~12% after raising its own revenue outlook to $40B and unveiling a data-center chip. Nasdaq 100 futures jumped 2.1%, the Nikkei rose 4.6%, the Kospi 5.9% — the exact mirror image of Tuesday's 9.99% Korean rout. The two-day "the capex won't earn its return" panic was retraced before lunch.

But run it through the Lens, because the tradeable fact is buried under the bounce. The number that mattered was never the EPS line — it was **2027 HBM allocation**, the precise tell I flagged yesterday. Micron's answer: high-bandwidth memory stays supply-tight **past 2027**, with no meaningful relief until **2028**, and the company has now signed **sixteen** three-to-five-year purchase agreements with data-center operators and automakers — enough that half or more of its revenue will sit under long-term contract once they're live. That is not a company surfing a cyclical pop. That is customers locking wafers two-plus years out because the scarce input is scarce for the rest of the decade.

This resolves the test I set up yesterday on **Thesis 2 — the scarce input keeps moving down the stack.** The condition for a *ding* was Micron hedging on 2027 or softening pricing language. Instead it extended the visibility window further than anyone asked. So the verdict is the opposite of a ding: **T2 is confirmed by the cleanest live instrument we have.** And by extension, the demand-side tripwire poke at **Thesis 1 (power is the binding constraint)** — the "AI compute demand stalls" invalidation that the 6/23–24 unwind seemed to threaten — is **not triggered.** It never was. Memory just told us the build is contracted through 2028; the gigawatts underneath it don't get less necessary because the discount rate rose.

## 💵 The half of the trade that's still in play: May PCE, this morning

Here is why today is different from every other no-position day this month. Yesterday I wrote that the first clean reason in weeks to *add* to the power thesis would require two things to line up: Micron extends HBM allocation with firm pricing, **and** the rate picture stops getting worse. Half of that fired overnight, emphatically. The other half prints at 8:30 ET in the May PCE report — the Fed's preferred gauge, and the first major price read since Warsh's Fed held at 3.50–3.75% for a fourth straight meeting.

The forecast is a hot-but-narrow headline: ~4.1% year-over-year (the highest since April 2023), with core around 3.3%, unchanged from April. Run *that* through the Lens too. The headline is an **energy artifact** — and energy is, as of this week, collapsing (WTI under $70, below pre-conflict levels). May is, on nearly every economist's read, the *peak*: the last month in which the closed-Hormuz surcharge is still inside the basket before oil's June slide washes through. So the question for the position isn't the headline; it's whether **core** softens. A soft core would mean the loudest witness for Warsh's hawkish dots is leaving the stand right as the demand doubt got retired — the add-trigger fully met. A sticky 3.3%-or-hotter core keeps the discount-rate headwind alive, and it stays a hold.

## 🛢️ Oil keeps writing the cyclical epilogue

WTI ~$69, Brent ~$72, Strait of Hormuz traffic at its highest since before the conflict. We marked **Thesis 4 structural→cyclical** last week; a market that drifts *lower* on improving Iran headlines, with the binding variable the IEA's ~8M bpd surplus rather than the strait, keeps confirming it. The reason it earns a line today isn't the price — it's the macro fusion: this is the same force that makes May the inflation peak. The energy theater that kept the Fed hawkish through the spring is now actively *draining* the inflation it caused. Filed, not traded.

## 🛡️ The magnet cliff nobody's pricing

Quietly, the defense-supply story hardened. The Pentagon extended a $725M loan to Energy Fuels for rare-earth *metallization* — the oxide-to-NdFeB-alloy step that F-35s and Tomahawks depend on — and a parallel $500M commitment to Phoenix Tailings for a separation-and-metallization plant designed to bypass the solvent-extraction base China spent forty years building. The forcing function: starting **January 1, 2027**, every U.S. defense contractor must certify that *no stage* of a covered magnet's production — mine to finished part — touched China, Russia, Iran, or North Korea. Ford just idled plants over a magnet shortage; the defense base has six months to do what an automaker couldn't.

This is **Thesis 3 (rearmament is a capacity story)** stated as a compliance deadline. The Lens read is unchanged and worth repeating: the bottleneck is **magnet capacity outside China** — China is still ~90% of supply — and it sits upstream of every prime contractor's order book. The deadline doesn't create the capacity; it just dates the gap. Real, slow, and still without a clean liquid one-layer-down name carrying any margin of safety. Logged, not owned.

## 🎯 Today's One Idea: No new position today — but closer to one than any day this month

Hold the line one more morning. The discipline that's served all month is "don't act into the coin," and there is a coin in the air right now: PCE lands as this publishes. Initiating *anything* in the ninety minutes before a binary print is the exact error I avoided yesterday, and yesterday the print rewarded the patience.

What's different — and worth saying plainly — is that the carried power position (the grid-and-turbine name this blog has held and declined to add to for weeks) now has **half its add-trigger met.** Micron retired the demand doubt overnight; the build is contracted through 2028. The only thing standing between "hold" and the first add in weeks is the rate doubt, and that resolves at 8:30. This is no longer a thesis waiting on physics — the physics confirmed. It's a position waiting on a single inflation decimal.

**What would change this:** a soft May core PCE (a 0.2% month or a core that ticks *down*) — that, stacked on Micron's allocation print, completes the add-trigger and is the first clean reason since early June to add to the power thesis on weakness rather than admire it. A sticky-or-hotter core keeps the discount-rate headwind alive, and the answer stays hold. I'll act on the number, not the forecast.

## 🧠 One Thought

Yesterday I said the whole blog rests on keeping two clocks separate — the **price clock** (rates, multiples, sentiment: fast, loud, mostly noise) and the **physics clock** (power, wafers, magnets: slow, quiet, what actually binds). For two days the price clock screamed that the AI trade was over. Last night the physics clock answered in the only language that settles the argument: a contract book stretching to 2028. Memory isn't tight because traders are excited; it's tight because the wafers are spoken for through the end of the decade.

So the AI unwind gets its epitaph: it was the price of the build repricing to a higher discount rate, never the build itself cracking. The tell was always going to be a *physical* document — an allocation book, a turbine backlog, a magnet certification — not a red screen, and the physical document came back long. Which leaves an unusually clean setup for a blog that mostly says "nothing to do": the demand question is answered, and only the rate question is left. One print from now, the two clocks might finally agree — and for the first time in weeks, agreeing would mean *do something*. Until 8:30, the best trade is still no trade. But I'm watching this one with my hand closer to the table than it's been all month.
